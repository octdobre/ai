🍲 Recipe Prompting Pattern

You often know a few steps of a plan but not the whole sequence, and a Large Language Model (LLM) asked for a plan from scratch may ignore the steps you already know. The recipe pattern gives the model your known steps and asks it to fill in the missing ones around them, like a recipe with only some of the ingredients listed.

Why it works

  • Your steps stay in place. The model builds the plan around what you already know, instead of replacing it with its own version.
  • Gaps get filled. The model adds the steps people often forget, such as an inspection, a booking or a backup.
  • Constraints shape the whole plan. A limit like “no more than 300 miles per day” applies to every step the model adds.

Examples

Each prompt states a goal and the steps you already know, then asks the model to fill in the rest. In the replies, your known steps are in bold.

1 Home purchase planning

UserI would like to purchase a house. I know that I need to perform steps make an offer and close on the house. Provide a complete sequence of steps for me. Fill in any missing steps.
ModelHere's the complete sequence of steps to purchase a house:
  1. Get pre-approved for a mortgage: meet with lenders to determine your budget and obtain a pre-approval letter.
  2. Find a real estate agent: select an experienced buyer's agent to help you through the process.
  3. Determine your requirements: list must-haves, nice-to-haves, and deal-breakers for your new home.
  4. Search for properties: tour homes that meet your criteria and budget.
  5. Make an offer: submit a written offer with your proposed price and terms.
  6. Negotiate terms: work with the seller to agree on price, contingencies, and closing date.
  7. Schedule a home inspection: hire a professional inspector to identify any issues with the property.
  8. Request repairs or renegotiate: address any issues found during the inspection.
  9. Secure financing: finalize your mortgage application and lock in your interest rate.
  10. Obtain homeowner's insurance: buy a policy required by your lender.
  11. Do a final walkthrough: check the property's condition before closing.
  12. Close on the house: sign all documents, transfer funds, and receive the keys to your new home.

The user's two steps stay in place as steps 5 and 12, and the model fills in the ten around them.

2 Road trip planning

UserI would like to drive to NYC from Nashville. I know that I want to go through Asheville, NC on the way and that I don't want to drive more than 300 miles per day. Provide a complete sequence of steps for me. Fill in any missing steps.
ModelHere's your complete road trip itinerary from Nashville to NYC via Asheville:

Before the trip
  1. Plan your route: Nashville → Asheville (about 295 miles) → Roanoke, VA (about 230 miles) → Washington, DC (about 240 miles) → NYC (about 225 miles).
  2. Book hotels in Asheville, Roanoke, and Washington, DC.
  3. Check your car: tire pressure, oil, and other fluids.
  4. Download offline maps, so navigation works without cell service.
  5. Pack a first aid kit, a phone charger, snacks, and water.
Day 1: Nashville to Asheville
  1. Leave Nashville early to avoid traffic.
  2. Stop for lunch around Knoxville, TN.
  3. Arrive in Asheville, check in, and explore downtown.
Day 2: Asheville to Roanoke
  1. Spend the morning in Asheville if you like.
  2. Drive to Roanoke, with time for scenic stops along the Blue Ridge Parkway.
  3. Arrive in Roanoke and rest.
Day 3: Roanoke to Washington, DC
  1. Head northeast through Virginia.
  2. Stop for lunch around Charlottesville.
  3. Arrive in Washington, DC, allowing for rush hour traffic.
Day 4: Washington, DC to NYC
  1. Leave early to avoid DC traffic.
  2. Have an E-ZPass or a way to pay tolls ready for the turnpikes.
  3. Arrive in NYC and head to your final destination or parking garage.

Every day's drive stays under the user's 300-mile limit, and Asheville is the first overnight stop.

3 Software deployment process

This prompt adds one more instruction, “Identify any unnecessary steps”, so the model can question a known step instead of only adding new ones.

UserI would like to deploy my web application to production. I know that I need to perform steps run tests and update DNS records. Provide a complete sequence of steps for me. Fill in any missing steps. Identify any unnecessary steps.
ModelHere's the complete deployment sequence:

Pre-deployment
  1. Complete code review: make sure all changes are reviewed and approved.
  2. Merge to the main branch: integrate your feature branch into the production branch.
  3. Run tests: run unit, integration, and end-to-end tests.
  4. Run security scans: check dependencies and code for vulnerabilities.
  5. Build the application: create production-ready build artifacts.
  6. Back up production: snapshot the database and the current deployment.
Staging deployment
  1. Deploy to staging: test the deployment in a production-like environment.
  2. Run smoke tests: check that critical features work in staging.
  3. Run load tests: make sure the application can handle expected traffic.
Production deployment
  1. Enable maintenance mode (optional): show a maintenance page during deployment.
  2. Deploy to production: push the new version to production servers.
  3. Run database migrations: apply any schema changes.
  4. Verify health checks: confirm all services are running and responding.
  5. Run post-deployment tests: test the critical paths in production.
  6. Watch logs and metrics for errors or performance issues.
  7. Disable maintenance mode: restore user access.
Post-deployment
  1. Monitor performance on your dashboards for 24–48 hours.
  2. Tell your team and users the deployment succeeded.
About updating DNS records
Updating DNS records is usually unnecessary unless you're changing hosting providers, moving to a new IP address, or changing your domain setup. If your infrastructure hasn't changed, you can skip this step.

The model keeps “Run tests” as step 3 but questions the DNS step instead of adding it to the list.
