🔤 How LLMs Predict the Next Word

A Large Language Model (LLM) doesn't look answers up. It predicts the next word, one token at a time, from probabilities it learned during training 🧠. Knowing this explains why the same prompt can give different answers, and why settings like temperature change the result.

Why it works

  • Training builds the probabilities. Trained on vast amounts of text, the model has learned which words tend to follow which, so its continuations sound natural. 📚
  • The prompt steers them. Every word you write changes the odds for the next one. Self-attention lets the model focus on the parts of the prompt that matter most.
  • Decoding picks from them. A setting such as temperature decides whether the model always takes the likeliest word or sometimes a less likely one.

How the next word is chosen 🎯

The prompt is split into tokens, turned into numbers, and passed through the model's transformer layers. The network outputs a score called a logit for every token in its vocabulary, and softmax turns those scores into probabilities. Decoding then picks the next token, either the most likely one (greedy decoding) or a sample using temperature, top‑k or nucleus sampling. 🎲 The chosen token is added to the text and fed back in, and the process repeats until the answer is complete.

Examples

Each example shows a prompt and the continuation the model produces under a different decoding strategy.

1 Greedy decoding

User · promptThe capital of France is
Model · completionParis

Greedy decoding always takes the most likely token, so this prompt gives the same completion every time.

2 Temperature = 1.0 (more diverse)

User · promptThe story begins at midnight and
Model · completionthe city exhales; lanterns flicker as footsteps scatter into alleys.

At a higher temperature, less likely words get a real chance, so each run can tell a different story.

3 Top‑k / nucleus sampling

User · promptA recipe for pancakes includes
Model · completionflour, eggs, milk, a pinch of salt, and butter.

Sampling only from the top few candidates keeps the list varied but sensible: no odd ingredients slip in.
