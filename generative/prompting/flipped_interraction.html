🔁 Flipped Interaction Pattern

You often don't know which details a good answer needs. Ask a Large Language Model (LLM) for a running plan, and it fills the gaps with guesses about your goal, your fitness and your schedule. Flipped interaction turns the conversation around: the model interviews you, one question at a time, and answers only once it knows enough.

Why it works

  • The model knows what to ask. It knows which details shape a running plan or a budget, so you don't have to work them out yourself.
  • Short questions are easy to answer. One question at a time gets quick, specific replies. A long form gets skipped or half-answered.
  • The answer fits you. Every detail in the result comes from your replies, not from the model's defaults.

Let the model interview you

Tell the model what you want at the end, ask it to question you one step at a time, and ask for the first question:

UserAsk me questions, one at a time, until you have enough information to write me a personalized running plan. Then show me the plan. Ask me the first question.
ModelWhat are you training for, and when is it?
UserA 10K race in ten weeks.
ModelHow much do you run now, and how long is your longest recent run?
UserAbout 15 km a week. My longest run is 6 km.
ModelHow many days a week can you train, and are there days you can't run?
UserFour days, but never on Wednesdays.
ModelAny injuries or pain I should plan around?
UserMy left knee hurts when I run downhill.
ModelThanks, that's everything I need. Here's your ten-week plan.

Tuesday: intervals. Start with 6 × 400 m at a hard pace and build to 5 × 1 km by week 7.
Thursday: an easy 4 to 6 km.
Saturday: an easy 3 to 5 km.
Sunday: a long run that grows from 6 km to 12 km by week 8.

Your weekly distance starts at about 16 km and builds to around 28 km, never more than 10% a week, and every fourth week is lighter. Choose flat routes and walk any steep descents to protect your knee. In the last ten days, cut your distance by half so you start the race fresh.

Every answer changed the plan: the race date sets its length, the current distance sets the starting point, Wednesdays stay free, and the routes avoid downhill running.

Before and after

The same request, without the interview and with it:

User · beforeWrite me a personalized running plan.
Before · no interview · ModelHere's a 12-week half marathon plan. Run four days a week: an easy run on Monday, intervals on Wednesday, a tempo run on Friday, and a long run on Sunday that builds from 10 km to 18 km.

The model guessed the race, the timeline, the fitness level and the training days, and got all four wrong for this runner.

One question at a time

Without “one at a time” and “Ask me the first question”, the model tends to send every question at once:

UserAsk me questions until you have enough information to write me a personalized running plan.
ModelSure! Please tell me: what distance you're training for, your race date, your current weekly distance, your longest recent run, how many days you can train, any injuries, your preferred surfaces, and whether you use a heart rate monitor.

Eight questions in one message turn the interview back into a form, and most people answer only the first few.

Prompt blueprint 🧭

Use this structure to reliably flip the interaction and control pacing:

User · templateYou are an expert [role]. Your task is to deliver [deliverable].

Interaction rules:
- Ask targeted clarification questions to gather only necessary information.
- Ask ONE question at a time. After each answer, decide whether more information is needed.
- If you have enough information, stop asking questions and produce [deliverable].
- Optional: Ask at most [N] total questions.

Begin now. Ask me the first question.

Putting it all together

A concise template that combines the interviewer role, pacing, and starter:

User · templateYou are an expert [role]. Your goal is to deliver [deliverable] that fits my needs and constraints. Ask ONE question at a time to collect only the necessary information. After each answer, reevaluate whether you can produce the deliverable. If you have enough information, stop asking and present it. Optional: Ask at most [N] questions.

Begin by asking me the first question.
