✍️ How Grammar Shapes LLM Outputs

A prompt's grammar strongly guides what a large language model (LLM) produces. Clear subjects, precise actions, and scoped qualifiers reduce ambiguity, leading to more reliable and controllable results. 🧠

Key grammar elements and their effects 📚

Each element below comes with a pair of prompts that differ only in that piece of grammar, each with the reply it gets.

1 Nouns (subjects, objects) 🧱

  • Anchor the model on specific entities or domains; increase precision.
  • Named entities trigger retrieval- or pattern-like responses around known topics.
User · ❌ VagueExplain this process.
ModelSure! This process involves several stages. First, the inputs are gathered, then they are transformed step by step, and finally the result is produced. Each stage depends on the one before it.

With no named subject, the model can only answer in general terms.

User · ✅ SpecificExplain the photosynthesis process in cyanobacteria for high-school students.
ModelCyanobacteria are tiny bacteria that make their own food from sunlight, just like plants do. A green pigment called chlorophyll a soaks up the light. That energy splits water molecules, which releases the oxygen we breathe. The bacteria then use the stored energy to turn carbon dioxide from the air into sugar, in a set of reactions called the Calvin cycle.

2 Verbs, mood, and tense 🚀

  • Imperatives (“list”, “summarize”, “compare”) prompt actions/outputs.
  • Interrogatives (questions) invite reasoning/explanations.
  • Tense anchors timeframe; present/future can change recommendations.
User · ❓ QuestionWhat are three risks of using web scrapers?
ModelWeb scraping can be risky in a few ways. Depending on the site, it may go against the terms of service, and some countries have laws about collecting personal data. There are also technical issues, since websites change often. Whether these risks matter depends on what you scrape and why.

A question invites an explanation, so the three risks are buried in prose.

User · 🛠️ ActionList three risks of using web scrapers.
Model
  • Legal: scraping can break a site's terms of service or data protection laws such as the GDPR.
  • Blocking: sites detect scrapers and block their IP addresses.
  • Breakage: a small change to the page layout silently breaks the scraper.

3 Adjectives and adverbs (qualifiers) 🎛️

  • Control tone, depth, and style: “brief”, “formal”, “step-by-step”, “creatively”.
  • Too many or conflicting qualifiers create ambiguity.
User · 🪢 NeutralSummarize the paper “Attention Is All You Need”.
ModelThis groundbreaking paper changed AI forever! The authors came up with an amazing idea called the Transformer, which is way better than anything before it. It's easily one of the most important papers ever written, and everyone should read it.

No tone or length was set, so the summary is short on facts and full of opinion.

User · 🎯 QualifiedSummarize the paper “Attention Is All You Need” briefly and objectively in 120–150 words.
ModelIn 2017, Vaswani and colleagues at Google proposed the Transformer, a model for sequence tasks such as translation that drops recurrence and convolution and relies only on attention. Each layer uses multi-head self-attention, which lets every word in a sentence weigh every other word directly, together with positional encodings that keep track of word order. Because attention can be computed in parallel, the model trains much faster than recurrent networks. On the WMT 2014 benchmarks, it scored 28.4 BLEU for English to German and 41.8 for English to French, ahead of earlier single models, and the large version trained in 3.5 days on eight GPUs. The authors also applied it to English constituency parsing, showing that the design works beyond translation.

4 Determiners and quantifiers 🔢

  • Constrain scope: “exactly”, “at least”, “each”, “every”.
  • Help the model size the output and avoid over/under-generation.
User · 🌫️ LooseProvide some bullet points about remote work.
Model
  • Remote work has many benefits like flexibility and no commute, but it also has downsides such as isolation, and how well it works depends on the company, the person, and the type of job.
  • Teams use lots of tools, such as Slack, Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Notion, and many others.
  • Some studies say productivity goes up, while others say it goes down.

“Some” sets no count and no length, so the bullets run long.

User · 📏 ConstrainedOutput exactly 5 bullet points about remote work, each under 15 words.
Model
  • No commute saves time and money.
  • Flexible hours suit different working styles.
  • Isolation can weaken team connections.
  • Clear written communication becomes essential.
  • A proper desk and chair matter more than at the office.

5 Pronouns and coreference 🔁

  • Unclear “it/this/they” leads to drift or hallucination.
  • Replace pronouns with explicit nouns or re-state the referent.

  • Add audience, domain, format, or platform (e.g., “for beginners”, “in Python”, “under 200 words”, “as a JSON array”).

7 Syntax and structure 🗂️

  • Lists, tables, and numbered steps reduce ambiguity and improve adherence.
  • Use explicit output schemas when needed.

Quick checklist for better prompts ✅

  • 🧱 Subject: Have you named the exact thing (noun) you care about?
  • 🚀 Action: Is the requested operation expressed with a strong imperative verb?
  • 🎛️ Qualifiers: Did you constrain tone, length, or depth appropriately?
  • 🔁 References: Did you resolve pronouns and remove ambiguity?
  • 📍 Scope/Format: Did you specify audience, domain, and output structure?

By aligning nouns, verbs, and modifiers with your intent, and by structuring the expected output, you make it easier for an LLM to produce accurate, useful responses.
