Role and guardrails ⏱️ – min read

👥 Audience Pattern

The Audience pattern designs prompts so a Large Language Model (LLM) produces output tailored to a clearly defined audience. By declaring who the output is for, you let the model choose the right depth, tone, terminology, structure, and examples. 🎯

Why it works 💡

Declaring the audience guides the model's choices about how to explain, not just what to explain:

  • Depth and terminology. The model picks the level of detail and the vocabulary that fit the reader.
  • Tone and structure. It shapes the voice and layout of the answer around who will read it.
  • Examples. It reaches for comparisons the reader already knows.

The result is clearer, more relevant, more trustworthy output, with fewer rounds of iteration. ✅

The prompt ✍️

Use a compact, audience-led instruction and an explicit ask. Both examples below follow this shape:

UserExplain how an llm works. Assume I am <audience>.

Same question, two audiences 🧭

Each example asks the same question. Only the declared audience changes, and the output (shown as an excerpt) changes with it.

1 Leonardo da Vinci 📜

UserExplain how an llm works. Assume i am Leonardo DaVinci.
Model · excerpt

📜 Most illustrious Leonardo, imagine a vast workshop of attentive apprentices trained upon countless manuscripts, letters, and treatises. An LLM is such a workshop in mathematical form: it has studied many examples of language and learned the tendencies by which one mark follows another. When you pose a question, it does not search a single book; rather, it predicts the most fitting next symbol again and again, line by line, until a complete answer stands—much as one infers the motion of gears from their teeth.

🧭 You may guide its hand as a patron sets a commission: request a tone (scholarly or plain), a length, or a style (notebook, letter, or decree). Know also its limits: it speaks confidently even when its certainty is low, so prudent tests—asking for sources or for a second, mirrored reasoning—help ensure sound results, as your own habit of sketching an invention from multiple angles reveals its truth.

2 A business traveler ✈️

UserExplain how an llm works. Assume I am a business man waiting in an airport terminal.
Model · excerpt

✈️ Picture a Large Language Model like the departures board predicting updates from patterns in thousands of past flights. It doesn’t look up one document; it keeps predicting the next most likely word—again and again—until a complete answer forms.

🧳 Plain terms
  • ⏱️ You ask a question; it continues with the most likely next words.
  • 🧭 Give direction (audience, tone, length) for faster, clearer results.
🛫 Handy while you wait
  • 📨 Draft a client email in a professional tone.
  • 📋 Summarize a 2‑page brief into key bullets.
  • ✅ Compare two vendors with pros/cons and a short recommendation.
⚠️ Limits
  • 🔎 Sounds confident even when unsure → skim for accuracy; ask for sources when possible.
  • 🔒 Don’t include sensitive details; use approved accounts and settings.

What changed

Both answers explain the same idea, that the model predicts the next word over and over, but each is built for its reader:

  • Metaphor. A workshop of apprentices and the motion of gears for Leonardo; a departures board for the traveler.
  • Structure. Flowing prose for Leonardo; short, scannable bullet sections for the traveler.
  • Practical advice. Leonardo is told to guide the model “as a patron sets a commission”; the traveler gets tasks to try while waiting.
