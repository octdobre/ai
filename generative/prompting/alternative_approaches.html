🔀 Alternative Approaches Pattern

Ask a Large Language Model (LLM) how to do something, and it usually gives you one answer: the first one it thinks of, not necessarily the one that suits you. The alternative approaches pattern asks the model to lay out several options, compare them, and help you choose.

Why it works

  • It widens the search. The model lays out several ways to reach the goal instead of stopping at the first one it thinks of.
  • Trade-offs become visible. Seeing what each option costs lets you decide, instead of accepting a default.
  • Your situation picks the winner. The model asks what matters to you, then recommends the option that fits.

Ask for options, then choose

Give the instruction once at the start. From then on, the model compares options and asks what matters before it recommends one:

UserFrom now on, when there are different ways to do what I ask, list the best ones as bullet points, each with one pro and one con. Then ask me questions to find the one that suits me.
ModelUnderstood. I'll list the options as bullet points with a pro and a con each, then ask what matters to you. What would you like to do?
UserHow should I back up my photos?
ModelThere are three good options:
  • External drive. Pro: cheap, and works offline. Con: you have to remember to copy your files, and a fire or theft can take it along with your computer.
  • Cloud storage, such as Google Photos or iCloud. Pro: backs up automatically and keeps a copy somewhere else. Con: costs a monthly fee once you pass the free space.
  • Both together. Pro: one copy at home and one off-site, the safest setup. Con: a little more to set up.
How many photos do you have, and would you rather pay monthly or copy files by hand?
UserAbout 40,000 photos, roughly 200 GB. I don't mind paying a little, but I always forget manual backups.
ModelThen go with cloud storage, since it runs without you. At 200 GB you would fill a 200 GB plan straight away, so choose a 2 TB plan from Google One or iCloud+, which costs about $10 a month in the US. If you want extra safety later, copy everything to an external drive once a year.

The model recommends only after it knows the size of the library and that this user forgets manual backups.

Before and after

The same question, sent without the instruction and with it:

UserHow should I back up my photos?
Before · no pattern · ModelCopy them to an external hard drive once a month.

One answer with no reasons, and it depends on the habit this user doesn't have.

After · with pattern · ModelThere are three good options:
  • External drive. Pro: cheap, and works offline. Con: you have to remember to copy your files, and a fire or theft can take it along with your computer.
  • Cloud storage, such as Google Photos or iCloud. Pro: backs up automatically and keeps a copy somewhere else. Con: costs a monthly fee once you pass the free space.
  • Both together. Pro: one copy at home and one off-site, the safest setup. Con: a little more to set up.
How many photos do you have, and would you rather pay monthly or copy files by hand?

Each option arrives as a bullet with one pro and one con, followed by questions, exactly as the instruction asked.

Another example: question the approach

Sometimes the best alternative is a different way to solve the problem behind your request:

UserFor anything I ask you to write, work out the problem I'm trying to solve, suggest at least one other way to solve it, and compare it with my approach.
ModelGot it. What should I write?
UserAn email reminding my team to fill in their timesheets.
ModelThe problem seems to be that timesheets arrive late, and your approach is a reminder email. Another option is a recurring calendar reminder every Friday at 3 pm. It takes no effort from you each week, but people tend to dismiss automatic reminders faster than a message from their manager.

Here is the email, in case you still want it:Hi team, a quick reminder to fill in your timesheets for this week by Friday at 5 pm. Thanks!

Prompt blueprint 🧭

Four variations of the instruction, each one aimed at a different kind of alternative:

I Basic alternative approaches

User · templateFrom now on if there are alternative ways to accomplish the same thing, list the best alternative approaches. Compare and contrast the alternatives and ask me questions to figure out which alternative approach suits me.

II Meta-prompting with alternatives

User · templateWhenever I ask you to write a prompt for me to accomplish a task, list what the task is, list alternative approaches for completing the task and then write a prompt for an LLM for each approach. Ask me what I want you to write a prompt for.

III Prompt wording alternatives

User · templateFor every prompt I give you, if there are alternative ways to word a prompt that I give you, list the best alternate wordings. Compare/contrast the pros and cons of each wording.

IV Problem-solution alternatives

User · templateFor anything that I ask you to write, determine the underlying problem that I am trying to solve and how I am trying to solve it. List at least one alternative approach to solve the problem and compare/contrast the approach with the original approach implied by my request to you.
