🔀 Alternative Approaches Pattern

The Alternative Approaches Pattern asks a Large Language Model (LLM) to find and present several ways to accomplish a task, instead of handing back a single solution. You see the trade-offs before you commit to one.

Why it works

  • It widens the search. The model explores different strategies, methodologies, or implementations that reach the same goal, rather than stopping at the first one it thinks of.
  • Trade-offs become visible. Comparing and contrasting the options shows what each one costs, so you make an informed decision instead of accepting a default.
  • Context decides the winner. When the best solution depends on factors that are not obvious up front, or the problem is unfamiliar, the alternatives let you pick the one that fits your constraints, preferences, and requirements.

Before and after

Each variant below is a standing instruction you give once at the start of a conversation. The example then sends the same user message twice: once without the instruction, and once with it.

1 Basic alternative approaches

prompt
From now on if there are alternative ways to accomplish the same thing, list the best alternative
approaches. Compare and contrast the alternatives and ask me questions to figure out which
alternative approach suits me.
UserHow should I back up my photos?
Before · no patternCopy them to an external hard drive once a month.
After · with patternThere are a few good options:
  • External drive: cheap and offline, but manual and lost if the drive fails.
  • Cloud storage: automatic and off-site, but needs a subscription.
  • Both: the safest, at the cost of more setup.
How many photos do you have, and would you rather pay monthly or do it by hand?

2 Meta-prompting with alternatives

prompt
Whenever I ask you to write a prompt for me to accomplish a task, list what the task is, list
alternative approaches for completing the task and then write a prompt for an LLM for each
approach. Ask me what I want you to write a prompt for.
UserWrite a prompt that helps me study for a history exam.
Before · no pattern“Summarize the key events of the unit I'm studying in bullet points.”
After · with patternTask: prepare for a history exam.
  • Summaries: “Summarize the key events of this unit in bullet points.”
  • Quizzing: “Ask me one exam-style question at a time and grade my answer.”
  • Timeline: “Build a timeline of this unit and explain how each event led to the next.”
Which approach should I develop further?

3 Prompt wording alternatives

prompt
For every prompt I give you, if there are alternative ways to word a prompt that I give you, list
the best alternate wordings. Compare/contrast the pros and cons of each wording.
UserTell me about climate change.
Before · no patternClimate change is the long-term shift in global temperatures and weather patterns…
After · with patternSome alternate wordings:
  • “Explain the main causes of climate change.” Focused, but leaves out effects and solutions.
  • “Summarize climate change for a 10-year-old.” Easy to read, but less detailed.
  • “List the strongest evidence for climate change.” Concrete, but narrow.

4 Problem-solution alternatives

prompt
For anything that I ask you to write, determine the underlying problem that I am trying to solve
and how I am trying to solve it. List at least one alternative approach to solve the problem and
compare/contrast the approach with the original approach implied by my request to you.
UserWrite an email reminding my team to fill in their timesheets.
Before · no patternHi team, a quick reminder to fill in your timesheets by Friday. Thanks!
After · with patternProblem: timesheets arrive late. Your approach: a reminder email.
  • Alternative: an automatic calendar reminder every Friday. It needs no effort each week, but is easier to ignore than a personal message.
Here is the email, in case you still want it: “Hi team, a quick reminder to fill in your timesheets by Friday. Thanks!”
