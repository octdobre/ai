Optimize inputs and completions ⏱️ – min read

✂️ Compressing Input Information

A Large Language Model (LLM) can only read what fits in its input context window. Paste in fifty full articles and most of them won't fit, so the model never sees the facts it needs. Compressing input information means extracting only the relevant sentences, each with an ID, so the important facts fit and the model can still cite where each one came from. ✂️🧠

Why it works

  • Only the relevant facts take up space. Dates, figures, causes and named actors stay; background and repetition go.
  • Every fact stays traceable. Each sentence keeps a stable ID such as W3.S112, so the answer can cite exactly where a claim came from.
  • The model can't stray. When it may use only the compressed sentences, it has nothing else to guess from.

Scenario ✂️

You want to answer history questions from dozens of Wikipedia articles, such as World War II, the French Revolution and the Industrial Revolution.

User · before · full articlesYou are a historian. Answer questions using ONLY the provided sources. Sources (full articles): - [W1] Wikipedia: World War II — full text - [W2] Wikipedia: French Revolution — full text - [W3] Wikipedia: Industrial Revolution — full text - [W4..W50] Additional history articles — full text Question: How did industrial capacity influence major European conflicts between 1789 and 1945?

Fifty full articles add up to far more text than most context windows can hold, so this prompt can't be sent.

User · after · compressedArtifacts: [W3.S112] British iron output exceeded 2.25M tons by mid‑19th century. [W1.S487] U.S. aircraft production rose from 6k (1940) to 96k (1944). [W2.S233] Price controls (1793) sought to curb wartime inflation in France. Question: How did industrial capacity influence major European conflicts between 1789 and 1945?
ModelIndustrial capacity shaped how long these conflicts could be fought and who could win them. In revolutionary France, the strain of war led to price controls in 1793 to curb inflation [W2.S233]. By the mid-19th century, Britain's iron output had passed 2.25 million tons, the industrial base behind its power [W3.S112]. In the Second World War, US aircraft production rose from about 6,000 in 1940 to 96,000 in 1944, turning factory output directly into military strength [W1.S487].

Every claim cites the sentence it came from, although the full articles never entered the prompt.

A practical workflow 🚀

1 🔎 Scope and select

Define the question, then keep only the sources that matter to it:

UserSystem: You are a research assistant. Identify which sources are relevant to the question.
User: Question: How did industrial capacity affect European conflicts (1789–1945)?
Sources: [W1..W50]
Return: IDs of relevant sources only, with 1‑line rationale per source.
ModelW1 (World War II): wartime production of aircraft, tanks and ships.
W2 (French Revolution): war economy, price controls and mass conscription from 1793.
W3 (Industrial Revolution): growth of British iron, coal and steam power.
W7 (Napoleonic Wars): the Continental System and Britain's ability to finance coalitions.
W12 (World War I): steel output and the shell crisis of 1915.

Five of the fifty articles survive. The other 45, such as the Renaissance and Ancient Rome, are dropped before any extraction starts.

2 ✂️ Extract key sentences (with citations)

Pull the sentences that carry dates, figures, causes or named actors, word for word, and attach an ID to each:

UserSystem: Extract only factual sentences from the given sources. No paraphrasing.
User: Return ≤ 20 sentences that best answer the question. Include source IDs.
Question: How did industrial capacity influence major European conflicts (1789–1945)?
Sources: <relevant snippets>
Format:
- [W3.S112] <sentence>
- [W1.S487] <sentence>
Model- [W2.S233] Price controls (1793) sought to curb wartime inflation in France.
- [W7.S118] The Continental System (1806) tried to weaken Britain by closing European ports to British trade.
- [W3.S112] British iron output exceeded 2.25M tons by mid‑19th century.
- [W12.S205] In 1913 Germany produced about 17.6M tons of steel, more than twice Britain's 7.7M.
- [W1.S487] U.S. aircraft production rose from 6k (1940) to 96k (1944).

Each article shrinks to one or two sentences, copied word for word and tagged with where they came from.

3 🧠 Summarize tightly (optional)

Condense the extracted sentences into a short synthesis, with the supporting IDs at the end:

UserWrite a 70‑token synthesis using ONLY these sentences. Append cited IDs.
[W3.S112] ...
[W1.S487] ...
[W2.S233] ...
ModelIndustrial output increasingly decided European wars. Revolutionary France fought inflation with price controls, Britain's iron industry grew past 2.25M tons, and by WWII U.S. factories went from 6k to 96k aircraft a year. [W2.S233, W3.S112, W1.S487]

Three sentences become one short paragraph that still names its sources.

4 📦 Assemble the bounded prompt

Combine the instructions, the question and the compressed sentences, and leave 10–15% of the window free for the answer: ✅

User · templateSystem: You are a precise historian. Use only the artifacts below. Cite IDs.
User question: <insert>
Artifacts (≤ 300 tokens):
[W3.S112] British iron output exceeded 2.25M tons by mid‑19th century.
[W1.S487] U.S. aircraft production rose from 6k (1940) to 96k (1944).
[W2.S233] Price controls (1793) sought to curb wartime inflation in France.
Constraints: If information is missing, say so and stop.

5 ✅ Answer with citations

Keep the model's reasoning inside the provided sentences, with a citation after each claim:

UserSystem: Answer using only artifacts. After each paragraph, list cited IDs.
User: How did industrial capacity influence major European conflicts (1789–1945)?
ModelIn the early period, war strained economies more than it drew on industry. France had to impose price controls in 1793 to curb wartime inflation.
Cited: W2.S233

By the mid-19th century, Britain's iron output had passed 2.25 million tons, giving it an industrial base no rival could yet match.
Cited: W3.S112

In the Second World War, production itself became a weapon: US aircraft output rose from about 6,000 in 1940 to 96,000 in 1944.
Cited: W1.S487

The artifacts say nothing about 1850–1939, so I can't describe how industry shaped the wars of that period.

Every paragraph ends with its sources, and the model says where the artifacts run out instead of filling the gap from memory.

Prompt templates

1 Faithful extraction (with citations)

User · templateYou are a precise analyst. Extract only facts that appear verbatim in the sources.
Return JSON with fields: claim, citation_ids[], source_span.
If uncertain, output an empty array.

2 Abstractive synthesis (bounded)

User · templateWrite a concise synthesis for the given question using ONLY the provided artifacts.
Hard limit: 200 tokens. Cite artifact IDs after each paragraph.
If information is missing, say what is missing and stop.
