🧩 Cognitive Verifier

Ask a Large Language Model (LLM) a broad question, and it answers right away, filling every gap with assumptions you never see. The cognitive verifier pattern makes the model list the smaller questions it needs answered first, then combine those answers into the final one.

Why it works

  • Missing information surfaces early. The sub-questions show what the model doesn't know before it commits to an answer.
  • Each part can be checked. Small questions have small answers, which are easier to verify than one big conclusion.
  • The final answer shows its reasons. It is built from the partial answers, so you can see which one led to which part of the result.

The prompt 🔧

Send these rules once. They change how the model handles every question after them:

UserWhen you are asked a question, follow these rules:

Generate a number of additional questions that would help more accurately answer the question.

Combine the answers to the individual questions to produce the final answer to the overall question.

Example: choosing a laptop 🤖

1 Generate sub-questions

UserWhich laptop should I buy for software development under $1,500?
ModelTo answer accurately, I need to know a few things:
  • What OS do you prefer or need (Windows, macOS, Linux)?
  • Are you compiling large codebases or running VMs or containers regularly?
  • Do you prioritize battery life over raw performance?
  • What screen size and resolution do you prefer?
  • Do you need particular ports (HDMI, Ethernet, SD) or upgradeable RAM and SSD?

2 Answer each one

User
  • OS: I prefer Windows or Linux.
  • Workload: medium projects and some Docker containers, no heavy GPU work.
  • Battery: all-day battery matters more than peak performance.
  • Display: 14 to 15 inches, at least 1080p.
  • Ports: USB-C and HDMI. Upgradeable RAM would be ideal.

3 Combine the answers

ModelLook at a 14 to 15 inch Windows laptop with 16 GB of RAM and a Ryzen 7 or Intel Core i7 U-series processor, such as the Lenovo ThinkPad T14 or the ASUS Zenbook 14. Both balance battery life with enough multi-core power for Docker, have USB-C and HDMI, and fit your budget. The ThinkPad is the better pick if upgradeable RAM matters, since many configurations have a free memory slot.

Every part of the recommendation traces back to one of the user's answers: the OS, the workload, the battery, the screen and the ports.

Before and after

The same question, sent without the rules and with them:

UserWhich laptop should I buy for software development under $1,500?
Before · no pattern · ModelGet a MacBook Air. It has excellent battery life, a great screen, and handles most development work easily.

The model assumed the operating system and the workload, and recommended a Mac to someone who prefers Windows or Linux.

After · with pattern · ModelTo answer accurately, I need to know a few things: What OS do you prefer or need? Are you running VMs or containers regularly? Do you prioritize battery life over raw performance?

Tailored examples 🎯

The same rules adapt to specific tasks by naming the sub-questions to ask and what the combined answer should produce.

🐞 Debug a web service

User · templateWhen you are asked to debug a failing web service, follow these rules:

Generate a number of additional questions about recent deployments, error logs, and upstream or downstream dependencies.

Combine the answers to isolate the failing component and propose a minimal rollback or targeted fix.

🗄️ Choose a data store

User · templateWhen you are asked to select a data storage option, follow these rules:

Generate a number of additional questions about data volume, access patterns, latency needs, and compliance constraints.

Combine the answers to recommend a store (e.g., relational vs. document vs. key-value) with a brief rationale.
