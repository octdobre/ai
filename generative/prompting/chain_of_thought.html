Decompose and verify ⏱️ – min read

⛓️ Chain-of-Thought Prompting

Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting elicits intermediate reasoning steps from a Large Language Model (LLM). Instead of jumping directly to the answer, the model articulates a brief sequence of thoughts, which strengthens its performance on tasks that require multi-step reasoning, planning, or decomposition.

When to use ⛓️

  • Multi-step problems. Clarify math, logic, and word problems that need several steps to solve.
  • Complex instructions. Decompose them into smaller steps you can verify one by one.

Example: making an omelette 🍳

The prompt asks for the task and for the reasoning behind it, laid out in steps. The model answers with a short, ordered chain instead of a single block of text.

UserTask: Please describe how to create an omelette. Briefly explain your reasoning behind it in steps.
Instruction: Briefly explain your reasoning behind it in steps.
Model · step-by-step reasoning
  • 1. Gather ingredients (eggs, salt, butter) and tools (nonstick pan, whisk, spatula).
  • 2. Whisk eggs with a pinch of salt until uniform; preheat pan on medium-low and melt butter.
  • 3. Pour eggs in; gently stir and push curds, then smooth surface to set a thin layer.
  • 4. Add optional fillings, fold the omelette, cook briefly until just set, and serve.

Example prompts you can reuse ⛓️

1 Think step by step

prompt
Think step by step. Use 2–5 concise, factual steps. Then provide a single "Answer:" line.

2 Short chain for verification

prompt
Show a short chain of thought for verification. Keep it under 5 lines, then give the answer.

3 Several paths, one checked answer

prompt
Generate three brief reasoning paths and answers; pick the answer that passes a units check.
Return only the final answer.
