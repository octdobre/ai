Decompose and verify ⏱️ – min read

⛓️ Chain-of-Thought Prompting

On a problem with several steps, a Large Language Model (LLM) that jumps straight to the answer often gets it wrong, and you can't see why. Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting asks the model to write out its reasoning step by step first, so each step builds on the last and mistakes show up where they happen.

Why it works ⛓️

  • Each step builds on a written result. The model works from what it has already written down, instead of holding the whole problem at once.
  • Mistakes become visible. A wrong step is easy to spot in a short chain, so you can see where an answer went wrong.
  • Complex instructions break into checkable parts. Math, logic, and multi-step tasks turn into small steps you can verify one by one.

Example prompt ⛓️

The prompt asks for the task and for the reasoning behind it in steps. The model answers with a short, ordered chain instead of a single block of text.

UserTask: Please describe how to create an omellete. Briefly explain your reasoning behind it in steps.
Instruction: Briefly explain your reasoning behind it in steps.
Model1) Gather ingredients (eggs, salt, butter) and tools (nonstick pan, whisk, spatula).
2) Whisk eggs with a pinch of salt until uniform; preheat pan on medium-low and melt butter.
3) Pour eggs in; gently stir and push curds, then smooth surface to set a thin layer.
4) Add optional fillings, fold the omelette, cook briefly until just set, and serve.

Before and after

A classic word problem where the quick answer is wrong:

User · beforeA bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost?
Before · no chain · ModelThe ball costs $0.10.

The intuitive answer: if the ball cost $0.10, the bat would cost $1.10 and the total would be $1.20.

User · afterA bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost? Think step by step, then give the answer.
After · with chain · Model1. Call the ball's price x. The bat costs x + $1.00.
2. Together: x + (x + $1.00) = $1.10, so 2x = $0.10.
3. x = $0.05.
Answer: the ball costs $0.05, and the bat costs $1.05.
