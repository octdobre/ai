Clarify and refine questions ⏱️ – min read

🔍 Question Refinement

Most questions are vaguer than they feel. A Large Language Model (LLM) answers what you typed, not what you meant, so a loose question gets a generic answer. Question refinement asks the model to suggest a sharper version of each question first, so you can switch to the better one before it answers.

Why it works

  • The model knows which details matter. It has seen countless similar questions, so it knows what changes the answer: the platform, the size, the deadline.
  • You stay in control. The model asks before switching, so you can accept the new question, adjust it, or keep your own.
  • You learn to ask better. After a few rounds, you start including those details yourself.

Ask for a better question

Send the instruction once at the start of the chat, as a pre-prompt. From then on, every question gets a sharper version first:

User · pre-promptWhenever I ask a question, suggest a better version of it and ask whether I want to use it instead.
ModelWill do. What's your first question?
UserHow do I make my website faster?
ModelA better question might be: “Which part of my homepage loads slowest on phones, and what single fix would help most?” Would you like to use it? If you tell me what the site is built with, I can be more specific.
UserYes. It's a WordPress blog with lots of photos.
ModelOn a photo-heavy WordPress blog, the slowest part on phones is almost always the first large photo. Run your homepage through PageSpeed Insights and check which element it names under Largest Contentful Paint. The single fix that helps most is converting your photos to WebP: WordPress already creates smaller copies of each image for phones, and a plugin such as EWWW Image Optimizer can do the conversion for you.

The refined question asked for one fix, and the user's reply added the platform, so the answer is short and specific.

Before and after

The same first question, answered directly and answered after refinement:

UserHow do I make my website faster?
Before · no refinement · ModelThere are many ways to speed up a website: optimize images, enable caching, minify CSS and JavaScript, use a content delivery network, reduce HTTP requests, upgrade your hosting, and lazy-load content below the fold.

The model lists every common fix, with no way to know which one matters for this site.

After · with refinement · ModelOn a photo-heavy WordPress blog, the slowest part on phones is almost always the first large photo. Another example: a health question

Refinement works just as well when you are trying to learn something. A broad question becomes one that has a clear answer:

UserIs coffee bad for you?
ModelA better question might be: “How much coffee a day is safe for a healthy adult, and who should drink less?” Would you like to use it?
UserYes.
ModelFor most healthy adults, up to about 400 mg of caffeine a day, roughly four cups of brewed coffee, is not linked to harmful effects, according to both the US Food and Drug Administration and the European Food Safety Authority. Pregnant women are advised to stay under 200 mg a day, and people with heart rhythm problems or anxiety may need to drink less because they react more strongly to caffeine.

FAQ

Is this the same as chain-of-thought (CoT)?

No. Chain-of-thought reveals intermediate reasoning steps. Question refinement focuses on clarifying what to answer before answering. They can be combined but serve different goals.

When should I skip refinement?

Turn refinement off for exact instructions, such as “convert this JSON to CSV”. There is nothing to sharpen, so the extra step only slows you down.
