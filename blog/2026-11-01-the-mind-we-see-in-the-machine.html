The mind we see in the machine

Today's AI talks so well that many people feel someone is on the other side. Some come to see it as a living being.

If AI is just math, why does it feel so undeniably alive?

Why it looks alive

For all of human history, only one thing could hold a conversation with you: another person. So when something answers in fluent language, your brain doesn't stop to check what it is. It assumes someone is there.

AI then adds a set of very human signals:

  • It says “I”. It talks about what it thinks and what it would like to help with, so it sounds like it has a self.
  • It reads your mood. Tell it you had a bad day and it answers gently. Share good news and it sounds glad.
  • It follows along. It picks up on what you said earlier, gets your jokes, and may ask about your dog weeks later.
  • It has a personality. Its tone stays consistent, so it starts to feel like someone you know.
  • It seems to be thinking. Replies appear word by word, as if someone were typing them.
  • It's always there. It never gets bored, tired or impatient with you.

None of this comes from a life being lived. AI was trained on huge amounts of human writing, so sounding human is exactly what it was built to do. It has no childhood, no body and no bad day of its own. It has learned that “I'm so sorry to hear that” is what tends to follow sad news.

AI sounds human because it was made from humans.

How we get tricked

This trick is older than today's AI. In 1966, MIT computer scientist Joseph Weizenbaum built ELIZA, a simple program that imitated a therapist by turning your sentences back into questions. It understood nothing. People confided in it anyway, and Weizenbaum's own secretary asked for the room to be cleared to talk to it in private.

Those people weren't foolish. Humans are built to see minds everywhere, because it's a good bet: mistaking a rustling bush for a predator costs little, while missing a real predator can cost your life. So we see faces in clouds and give names to our cars. Knowing that the thing on the screen is software doesn't switch this instinct off.

That is how the trick works. AI supplies the signals, and we supply the someone.
